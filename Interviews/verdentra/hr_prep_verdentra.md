# HR Interview Prep — Verdentra (Simplified Version)
> Focus on the bullet points. Don't memorize sentences. Just remember the structure and use your own words.

---

## Q1 — "Tell me about yourself."

**How to start:** "I've been working as a Full Stack Software Engineer for about 4 years now."

**The Bullet Points (Your Story):**
- **MI Synergy:** Started here doing backend work — Spring Boot APIs and databases.
- **Velou:** Moved here to build internal data tools using Vue.js and Node.js for retail brands.
- **Recurved:** Most recently worked here as a full stack dev. Managed multiple client projects at once using React, .NET, and AWS.
- **Why Verdentra:** Now, I want to focus more on the Microsoft stack (React + .NET) and work on bigger cloud and AI projects. That's why I'm here today.

---

## Q2 — "Why are you leaving your current job?" / "Why did you leave Recurved?"

**How to start:** "Recurved was great, but agency work is very fast-paced with lots of small projects."

**The Bullet Points:**
- I want to go deeper, not wider.
- I want to work on long-term products instead of jumping between quick client builds.
- I took a break to upgrade my .NET and cloud skills.
- I'm looking for a company doing serious work in cloud and AI, like Verdentra.

## Q3 — "Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?"

**How to start:** "In a few years, I want to be a strong senior engineer."

**The Bullet Points:**
- I want to be someone who can make architectural decisions, not just write code.
- I want to get really good at cloud architecture and distributed systems.
- I'd like to help mentor junior developers.
- I want to be the go-to person when the team has a hard technical problem.

---

## Q4 — "What is your biggest weakness?"

**How to start:** "Sometimes, I spend too much time trying to design the 'perfect' solution."

**The Bullet Points:**
- I tend to over-engineer things before I start coding.
- I might design a complex structure when a simple one would work fine.
- I'm getting better at asking myself: "Do we really need this to be this complex right now?"
- Usually, building the simple version first is the best way to go.

## Q7 — "Describe a conflict with a teammate and how you handled it."

**How to start:** "At Recurved, a teammate and I disagreed on how to handle user login for a project."

**The Bullet Points:**
- They wanted to use a quick third-party tool. I wanted to build it ourselves because the client had special rules.
- Instead of arguing, I suggested we write down the pros and cons of both options.
- When we looked at the facts together, we saw that my way fit the client's needs better.
- It taught me to always write down the facts before arguing about a solution.

---

## Q8 — "Tell me about a time you failed and what you learned."

**How to start:** "At Recurved, I built a filter feature for a client, but it wasn't what they wanted."

**The Bullet Points:**
- The project manager sent a short requirement, and I guessed what the client meant instead of asking.
- I built it, but the client said it was wrong because their data worked differently.
- I had to redo the work, which delayed the project.
- Now, if anything is unclear, I write down my understanding and ask "Is this correct?" before I write any code.

---

## Q9 — "How do you handle tight deadlines and pressure?"

**How to start:** "Working at an agency like Recurved, tight deadlines were normal."

**The Bullet Points:**
- First, I figure out what is absolutely necessary and what can be skipped for now.
- If I think a task will take longer than expected, I tell the project manager immediately, not at the last minute.
- I focus on one thing at a time and ask for help early if I get stuck.

---

## Q10 — "What motivates you?"

**How to start:** "For me, it's about building things that actually work well."

**The Bullet Points:**
- I get a lot of satisfaction from finishing a feature and seeing it solve a real problem.
- I love learning new things, which is why I've been studying Azure and Bicep recently.
- I really enjoy working with a team that cares about writing good, clean code.
